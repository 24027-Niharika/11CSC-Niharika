What is being done to protect these animals?

Across New Zealand, government agencies, scientists, and local communities are actively working to save endangered native wildlife from extinction. A major focus is ‘Predator Free 2050’, a nationwide goal to completely remove introduced pests(like rats, stoats, possums, and feral cats) so our native animals can safely breed and thrive. In NZ there are more than 110 predator-free islands.Some of the vulnerable species are moved to these predator-free islands. Additionally, targeted breeding and rehabilitation programs like Operation Nest Egg run by the Department of Conservation. Where they collect wild kiwi eggs and raise the chicks in safe environments until they are large enough(about 1. to 1.2kg) to defend themselves from predators like stoats, significantly increasing their chances of survival in the wild.

What can we do?

Everyday actions from individuals and communities make a huge difference in saving New Zealand's native wildlife. Here is how you can get involved and do your bit:

  • Set Up a Trap in Your Backyard: Join local community trapping networks or place a predator-safe trap in your garden to help control rats, stoats, and possums in your neighborhood.

  • Make Your Garden Wildlife-Friendly: Plant native trees and shrubs (like Kowhai or Flax) to provide natural food and shelter for native birds, lizards, and insects.

  • Keep Pets Responsible: Keep cats indoors or attached to a bell collar, and keep dogs on leashes in beaches, bush areas, and wildlife zones to prevent attacks on nesting birds and kiwi

  • Join a Local Conservation Group: Volunteer with community restoration projects, tree planting days, or local sanctuary groups working near you.

  • Report Wildlife Sightings: Use apps like iNaturalist or report sick, injured, or rare wildlife directly to the Department of Conservation hotline (0800 DOC HOT).
